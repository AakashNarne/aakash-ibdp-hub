# Economics HL — Assessed Work Mark Lessons

> [!tip] If you remember nothing else
> - Read the exact question demand and keep the answer in the right economic area; relevant economics used for the wrong question earns little.
> - Earn diagram marks with a correct, fully labelled diagram **and** an explanation of the mechanism and outcome.
> - Use precise terms, show calculation workings, and connect each real-world fact to the theory.
> - For longer responses, explain the theory, use the text/evidence throughout, and make evaluation and synthesis explicit.
> - Treat the scores below as examiner-commented examples, not guaranteed templates.

## What the comments rewarded

The IB booklet is for the course first assessed in 2022 and was updated in November 2025. It provides several useful contrasts:

- **HL Paper 1 Example 6 — 20/25.** The student explained both market structures, used fully labelled and explained diagrams, applied generally accurate terminology, and addressed the question. The 15-mark response developed the theory, used examples in context, explained diagrams and showed reasonably balanced synthesis. The comments note some flaws in terminology and diagram clarity: accurate, question-focused economics mattered more than decorative diagrams.
- **SL Paper 2 Example 3 — 37/40.** The student earned full marks for correct calculations with workings, correctly labelled diagrams, and explanations that connected a specific context to the economic mechanism. For example, fuel as an input raised firms’ production costs; new farming practices reduced costs and shifted supply, changing price and quantity. The 14/15 response explained intervention through economic theory, used the text throughout, and evaluated, though synthesis could be stronger. One lost mark came from describing “higher income” and “more productivity” without explaining a self-reinforcing cycle.
- **HL Paper 1 Example 4 — 6/25.** The response used microeconomics for a macroeconomic question, applied a diagram in the wrong context, omitted a real-world example, showed no synthesis or evaluation, and failed to answer which policy was most appropriate. Relevant vocabulary alone did not rescue it.

## Exam routine

1. **Decode the command term and scope.** Identify whether the question is micro, macro or global, what it asks you to explain/evaluate, and any named objective or policy choice.
2. **Select only relevant theory.** Define the key term accurately; do not substitute a memorized topic response for the question asked.
3. **Build the mechanism.** Explain each causal link: policy/input → incentives or costs → curve/market change → price, output, employment or welfare outcome.
4. **Make diagrams do work.** Label axes, curves, equilibrium, shifts and relevant outcomes; refer to the diagram in prose. Add only labels needed to answer the question.
5. **Use evidence deliberately.** For Paper 2, use the text through the response; identify the actor, context and evidence and explain what it demonstrates.
6. **Evaluate and synthesize.** Weigh conditions, trade-offs, time horizons and affected groups; make the final judgement answer the question rather than append a generic advantage/disadvantage list.

## Common mistakes / examiner traps

- **Wrong scope:** using micro theory for a macro question, or describing a policy without addressing the requested objective or best option.
- **Diagram without explanation:** an accurate graph does not replace the causal mechanism; a diagram in the wrong context does not earn application credit.
- **Vague causal chains:** terms such as “more income” or “higher productivity” need the links that explain how one leads to another.
- **Unsupported application:** give the specific case/text fact and connect it to theory; do not name-drop examples.
- **Calculation without workings or precision:** show the components/formula and sensible rounding/units where asked.
- **Evaluation as a label:** say who gains/loses, under what conditions and why; then weigh the result.

## Self-test

1. What did the 20/25 HL Paper 1 example do well, and what remained imperfect?
2. Why did the 6/25 response lose marks despite including some relevant terms and a diagram?
3. What made the high-scoring Paper 2 diagrams useful rather than decorative?
4. What additional reasoning was missing from the “higher income → more productivity” claim?
5. What must a Paper 2 evaluation do beyond listing advantages and disadvantages?

> [!question]- Answers
> 1. It answered the question, explained theory, used accurate labelled diagrams, relevant examples and balanced synthesis; terminology had some flaws and diagrams could be clearer.
> 2. It answered the wrong macro/micro scope, used the diagram in the wrong context, omitted a real-world example and evaluation/synthesis, and did not recommend the most appropriate policy.
> 3. They were fully labelled and paired with prose explaining the mechanism and outcome in the specific context.
> 4. The causal links that make productivity and income reinforce each other were not explained.
> 5. It should weigh evidence, conditions and trade-offs and reach a supported judgement that answers the question.
