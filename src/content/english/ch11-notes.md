# English Paper 1 — Opinion Article

## Purpose and argument
An opinion article advances an identifiable interpretation or position on an issue. Its purpose may be to persuade, challenge, criticize, defend, or reframe. Identify the central claim, intended audience, and context. Then map how reasons and evidence support that claim; do not confuse the topic with the writer’s argument about it.

## Trace the line of reasoning
Mark the thesis or implied position, main reasons, examples/evidence, counterargument or concession, rebuttal, and conclusion. Notice whether the article builds logically, shifts tone, anticipates reader objections, or relies on assertion. A clear structure can make an argument persuasive, but it does not by itself make the evidence sound.

## Choices to analyse
- **Headline and opening:** can state a position, provoke, frame a question, or create urgency. Ask what assumptions it invites the reader to accept.
- **Voice and stance:** first person, direct address, inclusive pronouns, certainty/hedging, evaluative diction, irony, humour, or indignation construct ethos and relationship.
- **Appeals and evidence:** personal experience, expert sources, statistics, analogy, historical example, or moral principle. Analyse how the evidence supports the claim and what qualifications it needs.
- **Diction and syntax:** loaded labels, repetition, parallelism, rhetorical questions, short emphatic sentences, or long qualified reasoning shape tone and emphasis.
- **Counterargument:** a fair concession may build credibility; a simplified opponent may create a straw man. Explain how the opposing view is represented and answered.
- **Structure and progression:** order of reasons, transitions, paragraphing, contrast, and conclusion determine how the reader follows the case.
- **Publication and audience:** byline, platform, genre conventions, and presumed shared values can affect credibility and appeal. Do not assume that publication alone proves authority.

## Evaluate persuasiveness
Judge how effectively the reasoning and language serve the intended audience. Consider whether claims are supported, counterpositions handled fairly, and the conclusion follows from the argument. A forceful tone may energize sympathetic readers but alienate undecided readers; frame this as a reasoned possibility, not a universal response.

## Common traps
- Summarizing the writer’s argument without analysing how it is constructed.
- Calling every strong word “emotive language” without identifying its connotations or purpose.
- Treating statistics as self-explanatory; ask what they measure and whether they prove the claim.
- Equating a concession with balance or an opinion with unreliability.
- Claiming the audience is persuaded without considering evidence and audience position.

## Exam checklist
Can I state the thesis accurately? Have I followed claim → evidence → reasoning? What voice and appeals shape the reader’s position? How is opposition handled? What is the article’s strongest persuasive move and its limitation? Does each paragraph answer the guiding question?
