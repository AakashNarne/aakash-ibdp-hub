# English Paper 1 — Comic Strip

## Form and purpose
A comic strip tells a short story or delivers humour, social observation, or criticism through a sequence of panels. Meaning develops across time: the reader infers movement and change between still images. Analyse the sequence, not just the final panel or individual drawings.

## Read panel by panel
Track who appears, what changes, what is repeated, and what information is withheld until later. Notice reading order, panel size, borders, gutters (the spaces between panels), speech/thought balloons, captions, sound effects, and the final beat. Ask what the reader must infer between panels and how pacing affects surprise or emphasis.

## Choices to analyse
- **Panel sequence and pacing:** many small panels can slow a moment; a large panel may emphasize a reveal; an abrupt cut can create surprise. Base the claim on the actual sequence.
- **Gutters and transitions:** the gap invites the reader to supply an action, time jump, or causal link. Explain how that inference contributes to humour or meaning.
- **Repetition and variation:** repeated framing or wording establishes a pattern; a final change can expose a contradiction or deliver a punchline.
- **Dialogue and captions:** voice, register, brevity, interruption, or mismatch between words and image can create characterization, irony, or tension.
- **Expression, gesture, and gaze:** posture and facial expression can contradict spoken words or reveal a character’s attitude.
- **Visual style and composition:** line, scale, perspective, setting, background detail, and contrast determine what receives attention and how the tone feels.
- **Punchline and implied criticism:** identify the expectation built by earlier panels and how the ending revises it. If there is social commentary, explain how the joke carries the criticism.

## Analytical paragraph pattern
Make a claim about the strip’s developing idea or effect. Refer to at least two connected panels where relevant; explain the change, the reader’s inference, and the resulting humour or comment. Link it to the guiding question. Avoid listing every panel unless the sequence itself is the point.

## Common traps
- Treating a comic strip as a set of unrelated illustrations.
- Describing the punchline but not explaining the expectation it overturns.
- Ignoring the reader’s inference across the gutter.
- Calling any surprise “irony” without identifying the contrast.
- Assuming the joke’s target is obvious when context or wording suggests another reading.

## Exam checklist
What changes across the panels? What expectation is established? Where does the shift occur? How do words and images cooperate or conflict? What must the reader infer? If the strip criticizes something, who or what is the target?
