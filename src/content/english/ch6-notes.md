# English Paper 1 — Political Cartoon

## Purpose and context
A political cartoon comments on a public issue, policy, institution, event, or powerful figure. It may criticize, satirize, expose contradiction, or invite reflection. Before interpreting, identify what issue is depicted and what contextual knowledge the cartoon expects. Use only context provided by the text or reliably known; do not build the whole reading on an unverified news event.

## A visual reading sequence
1. Describe the literal scene: people/objects, action, setting, labels, speech, and composition.
2. Identify distortions or relationships: exaggeration, scale, contrast, juxtaposition, omission, or an unexpected pairing.
3. Infer the cartoonist’s position and the target of the comment.
4. Explain how visual and verbal choices invite the reader to judge the issue.

## Choices to analyse
- **Caricature and exaggeration:** enlarged features, distorted body, or disproportionate objects can ridicule, condemn, or make power visible. Name what is exaggerated and why it matters.
- **Symbol and visual metaphor:** an object may stand for an idea or institution. Establish the link from labels, context, or recognizable convention rather than assuming symbolism.
- **Juxtaposition and contrast:** opposing figures, settings, or scales can expose inequality, hypocrisy, conflict, or cause and consequence.
- **Labels and captions:** labels anchor an image’s meaning; a caption may clarify, narrow, or complicate it. A short line can create irony when it conflicts with what is shown.
- **Irony, parody, and satire:** explain the gap between what is said and what is shown, or between an expected ideal and the depicted reality. Identify who is criticized and what assumption the humour depends on.
- **Composition and gaze:** foreground/background, centrality, direction of gaze, speech bubbles, framing, and empty space control attention and position the reader.
- **Line, style, and tone:** sparse line, visual detail, grotesque distortion, or understatement can make commentary playful, harsh, absurd, or restrained.

## Build a defensible interpretation
State a central claim about the cartoon’s viewpoint, then support it with linked details. Distinguish literal description from interpretation. If more than one reading is possible, acknowledge the ambiguity and explain which reading the labels/composition best support. Evaluate whether the cartoon’s criticism is clear and persuasive, or whether it relies heavily on shared context.

## Common traps
- Retelling the scene without analysing its comment.
- Assigning a symbol meaning without showing why.
- Saying “the cartoonist uses irony” without explaining the contradiction.
- Assuming a cartoon is neutral or that it criticizes every person shown equally.
- Overstating context not supplied in the text.

## Exam checklist
What is the target of the critique? What visual detail makes the stance legible? How do caption/labels interact with the drawing? What prior knowledge is required? Does the interpretation answer the prompt, and have I supported it with visible evidence?
