# English Paper 1 — Public Service Announcement (PSA)

## Purpose and audience
A PSA seeks public awareness or a behaviour change in relation to a social, health, safety, or environmental issue. Identify the behaviour being discouraged or encouraged, who is responsible for the message, and who can realistically act. A PSA may target an individual, a community, or an institution; do not reduce every message to “raising awareness.”

## Persuasive structure
Look for the problem or risk, the consequence, the proposed action, and the source of authority. Does the text explain a practical response, or mainly make the audience feel concern? Is the action immediate, achievable, and specific? A strong analysis considers both urgency and efficacy: does the PSA make the issue matter while showing that action can help?

## Choices to inspect
- **Image and framing:** close-up, distance, point of view, focal subject, crop, scale, contrast, empty space, before/after image, or a visual metaphor. Explain how the image makes the issue personal, distant, urgent, preventable, or systemic.
- **Emotional appeals:** fear, empathy, guilt, hope, humour, pride, or solidarity. Identify the detail that creates the appeal and whether it motivates or risks alienating the intended audience.
- **Language:** imperative (“Stop…”, “Act now”), direct address, inclusive pronouns, rhetorical questions, repetition, contrast, short sentences, statistics, or a memorable slogan. Explain the tone and how it positions the viewer.
- **Call to action:** hotline, website, instruction, pledge, donation request, or a small behavioural step. Its placement, visibility, and specificity affect whether concern can become action.
- **Authority and trust:** sponsor logo, expert, institution, testimonial, source/date, or evidence. Ask how credibility is built and whether the evidence is clearly attributed.
- **Design hierarchy:** headline, image, explanatory copy, sponsor, and action prompt may compete or reinforce one another. Track reading order and legibility.

## Analysis and evaluation
Explain how choices support the PSA’s purpose for a particular audience, then evaluate limits. A shocking image may create urgency but offer little practical agency; a clear instruction may be actionable but visually easy to miss. These are text-specific possibilities, not automatic effects. Avoid claiming that one image will make every viewer feel the same way.

## Common traps
- Calling every non-commercial poster a PSA without establishing purpose and sponsor/context.
- Naming “pathos” or “fear” without describing the evidence and mechanism.
- Ignoring the proposed action or whether it is feasible.
- Treating a statistic as automatically trustworthy; inspect source, date, framing, and what it measures.
- Assuming a dramatic image is effective simply because it is dramatic.

## Exam checklist
State issue, purpose, audience, and desired action. Analyse at least one interaction between image and words. Explain how hierarchy directs attention. Evaluate both persuasive force and possible limitation. Keep each point tied to the guiding question.
