# English Paper 1 — Print Advertisement

## Purpose and reading stance
A print advertisement promotes a product, service, experience, brand, or idea. It does more than describe an offer: it constructs a reason to want it, often linking it to identity, belonging, beauty, status, convenience, safety, pleasure, or social responsibility. Do not assume every ad sells a physical product; identify the specific action or attitude it wants from its target audience.

## Read the whole page first
Record the advertiser/brand, product or cause, likely audience, publication/context if supplied, and apparent call to action. Then map the visual hierarchy: what catches attention first, second, and last? Notice what is large, central, brightly coloured, isolated by white space, repeated, or placed near the logo. Consider what is omitted or difficult to read.

## Choices worth analysing
- **Image and product placement:** scale, angle, crop, lighting, gaze, setting, models, props, and the relationship between product and people. Ask what lifestyle or identity is attached to the product.
- **Slogan and copy:** imperative verbs, second-person address, short phrases, rhyme, repetition, puns, evaluative adjectives, claims, and emotional or technical vocabulary. Explain what the wording asks the audience to believe or do.
- **Typography and hierarchy:** font, size, weight, spacing, contrast, and alignment establish brand personality and guide reading order.
- **Colour and composition:** palette, contrast, symmetry, diagonals, framing, negative space, and visual balance can create emphasis or associations—but colour meanings depend on context.
- **Branding and credibility:** logo, endorsement, testimonial, certification, price, guarantee, or fine print may construct trust or authority. Ask whether the ad gives evidence for its claims and how much is left unqualified.
- **Text-image relationship:** copy may anchor an image, add a joke, explain a visual metaphor, or contradict the image to create irony. This relationship is often more significant than either element alone.

## Analytical paragraph pattern
Make one claim about the ad’s persuasive strategy. Cite an exact visual or verbal detail; explain how it directs attention or creates an association; then connect that effect to the audience and intended action. Example frame: “By placing ___ beside ___, the advertiser links ___ with ___; this may encourage ___ to see the product as ___, supporting the ad’s aim to ___.” Adapt it to the actual text rather than filling in a memorized formula.

## Common traps
- Listing devices without explaining their persuasive function.
- Calling an ad effective without judging evidence, audience fit, or clarity of the call to action.
- Assuming the audience is “everyone” or that all viewers respond identically.
- Treating an attractive image as proof of a product claim.
- Ignoring small print, source, price, or the logo’s role in the layout.
- Importing generic symbolism (for example, “red means danger”) without evidence from the ad.

## Exam checklist
Can I state what is being promoted and what response is sought? Have I selected the most important choices rather than every detail? Have I explained image-copy interaction, hierarchy, and audience positioning? Does each paragraph connect to the guiding question? Have I distinguished a claim made by the ad from a fact independently established?
