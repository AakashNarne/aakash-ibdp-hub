# English Paper 1 — Infographic

## Purpose and form
An infographic combines words, data, and visual design to explain, summarize, or persuade. Decide which function dominates in this particular text. A polished graphic is not automatically accurate or neutral: selection, scale, labels, comparisons, and omissions shape how data is understood.

## Map its information architecture
Start with headline, source/producer, date, and overall claim. Track reading order and how sections are grouped. Identify the intended route through the page: headline → claim → evidence → conclusion/call to action, or another sequence. Notice where icons, illustrations, charts, labels, and explanatory text guide the eye.

## Choices to inspect
- **Data and comparison:** what is counted, compared, or converted? Check units, denominator, time period, baseline, and whether figures are directly comparable. Analyse the framing; do not independently treat claims as verified facts.
- **Chart form and scale:** bars, circles, maps, timelines, pictograms, and proportional images each make some relationships easier to see than others. Examine axes, intervals, truncation, labels, and visual proportionality.
- **Typography and hierarchy:** headline size, bolding, colour, spacing, and placement establish importance and sequence.
- **Icons and images:** they may make an abstract quantity tangible, stand for a category, humanize an issue, or simplify it. Ask whether the visual clarifies or oversimplifies the data.
- **Colour and grouping:** colour coding can sort information and connect sections, but can also imply value or contrast. Explain what it does in the specific graphic rather than assigning universal meanings.
- **Copy, tone, and call to action:** direct address, slogan, short explanations, and evaluative wording may turn information into persuasion.
- **Source, date, and attribution:** these affect authority and context. Small or absent sourcing may limit how confidently a reader can assess the claim.
- **Omissions and emphasis:** ask what comparison or qualification would change the takeaway and whether the graphic foregrounds one measure over another.

## Explain word-image interplay
Choose one key claim and explain how the written statement, data display, and nearby image reinforce or complicate it. For instance, a human-scale illustration may make a large number imaginable; a headline may tell the reader how to interpret a chart; a visual contrast may turn a statistic into a moral appeal. Be precise about what is visible and avoid inventing the creator’s intent.

## Common traps
- Listing statistics without explaining their role or design.
- Treating colour as decoration or universal symbolism.
- Claiming a graphic proves something without checking units, source, or comparison.
- Ignoring visual hierarchy and the order in which information appears.
- Saying “the infographic appeals” repeatedly without identifying the audience or mechanism.

## Exam checklist
Can I state the main claim and purpose? Have I analysed the chart choice, scale, units, labels, sourcing, and hierarchy where relevant? Have I connected image and data? Have I distinguished the infographic’s claim from verified fact? Does my interpretation stay within the evidence?
