# English Paper 1 — Feature Article

## Purpose and form
A feature article explores a person, issue, event, or trend in depth for a readership. It may inform, interpret, entertain, or persuade, and can combine reporting with narrative techniques. Unlike a straight news report, it often develops a scene, voice, theme, or human perspective. Text types overlap, so identify the article’s actual method rather than relying only on its label.

## Follow the article’s movement
Read headline, subheading, byline, opening, section breaks, quotations, images/captions, and ending. Ask how the article moves from an anecdote or scene to broader context, evidence, or reflection. Notice what the opening withholds and how the conclusion leaves the reader thinking or acting.

## Choices to analyse
- **Headline and standfirst/deck:** framing, wordplay, question, quotation, or evaluative language establishes the angle and attracts a particular reader.
- **Lead/opening:** a scene, anecdote, striking fact, question, or direct claim controls entry into the topic. Explain what it makes salient and what context is delayed.
- **Narrative and structure:** chronology, flashback, contrast, shifts between individual story and wider issue, or section headings shape emphasis and pace.
- **Voice and register:** first/third person, conversational or formal diction, humour, authority, uncertainty, and stance construct a relationship with the reader.
- **Sources and quotations:** attribution, expert testimony, personal testimony, and countervoices build credibility or emotional connection. Consider who is quoted, who is absent, and how quotations are framed.
- **Evidence and detail:** statistics, description, examples, and technical explanation can lend authority or make an issue tangible. Analyse selection and context; do not assume inclusion equals balance.
- **Images, captions, and layout:** these can humanize, contextualize, dramatize, or narrow interpretation. Analyse their relationship to the article rather than treating them as decoration.
- **Ending:** a return to the opening, a new perspective, recommendation, or unresolved question can shape the article’s final significance.

## Analysis focus
A strong paragraph explains how a particular choice helps the article’s angle develop. For example, trace how a personal opening introduces a wider concern, then show how evidence or later voices broaden or complicate it. Evaluate whether the structure sustains interest and supports understanding for the intended readership.

## Common traps
- Calling an article “biased” merely because it has a viewpoint.
- Summarizing anecdotes instead of analysing why they are selected and placed.
- Treating quotations or statistics as automatically reliable or representative.
- Assuming a feature must be neutral, or that a personal lead proves the whole article is opinion.
- Ignoring the article’s structure, source attribution, captions, and ending.

## Exam checklist
What is the article’s angle and audience? How does the lead establish it? How are story, evidence, and voices arranged? What does the writer include or leave out? How do language and layout guide interpretation? Does my analysis address the exact prompt?
