# Hindi B SL — Vocabulary, मुहावरे and Criteria Checklist

Companion to [[Hindi B SL — Identities and Social Organization — Notes]]. Use this as a growing active-recall bank: learn words in phrases, then adapt them to the task, stimulus and audience. Spellings/meanings below are revision aids; verify unfamiliar idioms with your teacher before using them in an assessed response.

> [!important] About “Criterion D”
> For the **current Hindi B SL assessment described in this vault**, there is no official Criterion D. Paper 1 uses **A Language, B Message, C Conceptual understanding**. The Individual Oral uses **A Language, B1 Message (visual stimulus), B2 Message (conversation), C Interactive skills**. Paper 2 is assessed with question-specific markschemes, not A–D criteria. The “D” checklist below is therefore a useful **personal proofread**, not an IB criterion. If your teacher gave you a separate A–D rubric, use that rubric and ask them what D means.

## If you remember nothing else

- Choose the right **message** first; sophisticated vocabulary cannot rescue an irrelevant answer.
- Make the text type, audience, context and purpose clear from the opening and register.
- Prefer accurate, varied Hindi to risky words, forced idioms or a memorised paragraph.
- Develop points with a reason, example or consequence; link ideas with precise connectors.
- In the oral, respond to the stimulus and examiner, not to a pre-learned speech.

### Useful connectors and stance phrases

| Function | Hindi phrases | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Add | इसके अलावा, साथ ही, इसके अतिरिक्त | Avoid stacking several near-identical connectors. |
| Sequence | सबसे पहले, इसके बाद, अंत में | Keep the order of events/arguments logical. |
| Give a reason | क्योंकि, इसका कारण यह है कि… | **क्योंकि** introduces a reason; do not use it as a result marker. |
| Show a result | इसलिए, परिणामस्वरूप, इसके फलस्वरूप | Make sure the result actually follows from the reason. |
| Contrast / qualify | लेकिन, हालाँकि…, फिर भी…, दूसरी ओर… | **हालाँकि** often needs a contrasting follow-up. |
| Give an example | उदाहरण के लिए, जैसे कि… | Explain how the example supports the point. |
| Give an opinion | मेरे विचार से…, मेरी राय में…, मुझे लगता है कि… | Support opinions with a reason or example. |
| Conclude | कुल मिलाकर…, अंततः…, निष्कर्षतः… | Add a conclusion, not a new unrelated argument. |

## मुहावरे: short, useful list

A **मुहावरा** is an idiomatic expression whose intended meaning is not simply the literal meaning of its words. Use one only when it naturally fits the context; never insert one just to sound advanced.

| मुहावरा | अर्थ | Safe example |
|---|---|---|
| कमर कसना | तैयार होना; पूरी तैयारी करना | परीक्षा से पहले विद्यार्थियों ने मेहनत के लिए कमर कस ली। |
| खून-पसीना एक करना | बहुत मेहनत करना | किसान अच्छी फसल उगाने के लिए खून-पसीना एक करते हैं। |
| एक तीर से दो शिकार करना | एक काम से दो लाभ पाना | साइकिल से स्कूल जाकर मैं पैसे बचाता हूँ और व्यायाम भी करता हूँ—एक तीर से दो शिकार। |
| ईद का चाँद होना | बहुत दिनों बाद दिखाई देना | व्यस्तता के कारण मेरा मित्र आजकल ईद का चाँद हो गया है। |
| आँखें चुराना | सामना करने या उत्तर देने से बचना | गलती के बारे में पूछने पर वह आँखें चुराने लगा। |
| दाँत खट्टे करना | बुरी तरह परास्त करना / कड़ी चुनौती देना | हमारी टीम ने प्रतियोगिता में विरोधी टीम के दाँत खट्टे कर दिए। |
| दिन-रात एक करना | लगातार बहुत मेहनत करना | उसने परीक्षा की तैयारी के लिए दिन-रात एक कर दिया। |
| अपने पैरों पर खड़ा होना | आत्मनिर्भर होना | कौशल सीखकर युवा अपने पैरों पर खड़े हो सकते हैं। |
| सोने पर सुहागा | अच्छी बात में एक और अच्छी बात जुड़ना | पुस्तकालय में मुफ्त इंटरनेट भी है—यह सुविधा सोने पर सुहागा है। |
| दाल न गलना | योजना या कोशिश सफल न होना | नियम तोड़ने की उसकी कोशिश वहाँ दाल नहीं गली। |
| हाथ पर हाथ धरे बैठना | कुछ न करना; निष्क्रिय रहना | प्रदूषण की समस्या पर हाथ पर हाथ धरे बैठने के बजाय हमें कदम उठाने चाहिए। |
| आकाश-पाताल एक करना | बहुत प्रयास करना / हर सम्भव कोशिश करना | लापता दस्तावेज़ खोजने के लिए कर्मचारियों ने आकाश-पाताल एक कर दिया। |

> [!warning] Idiom accuracy
> An idiom can sound unnatural if its subject, tone or situation does not fit. Avoid insulting or gendered expressions (for example, “चूड़ियाँ पहनना”), and do not repeat an idiom from a source if you are unsure of its sense. One accurate idiom is better than several forced ones.

## What to look out for in each criterion

### Criterion A — Language

**Paper 1:** demonstrate accurate, understandable Hindi with appropriate range. **Individual Oral:** sustain communication with clear, accurate and varied language.

Before submitting/speaking, check:
- **Gender and number:** लड़की जाती है; लड़का जाता है; लड़कियाँ जाती हैं; लड़के जाते हैं.
- **Agreement in noun phrases:** परिवार **की** परंपरा; परिवार **के** सदस्य; परिवार **का** महत्व. का/की/के agree with the noun that follows.
- **Verb tense and person:** keep the time frame consistent; change tense when the time changes.
- **Postpositions:** use के साथ, के लिए, से, में, पर appropriately; the noun/pronoun before them may change form (मुझसे, उसके साथ).
- **Sentence control:** mix short clear sentences with some connected/complex ones; avoid long run-ons.
- **Vocabulary range:** use topic-specific words precisely; do not repeat the same vague words (अच्छा, बुरा, चीज़) throughout.
- **Register and politeness:** use आप/आपका and suitable formal expressions for an authority; do not switch casually between आप and तुम.
- **Devanagari and spelling:** check मात्रा, अनुस्वार/चंद्रबिंदु where relevant, and word boundaries. If unsure of a rare word, choose a simpler one you can spell.
- **Oral fluency:** speak at a measured pace, pronounce clearly, and repair a mistake without abandoning the point.

### Criterion B — Message (Paper 1) / B1 and B2 — Message (Individual Oral)

The exact descriptor depends on the component. Keep the response relevant, developed and easy to follow.

**Paper 1 — Message:**
- Answer the chosen task and all its bullet points; underline the command/action words in the prompt before planning.
- Stay within **250–400 words** for SL Paper 1 (check the current instructions on your paper).
- Develop each main point: claim → explanation/reason → example or consequence.
- Keep paragraphs focused and sequence ideas logically; do not drift into a memorised theme essay.
- Include a clear opening and ending that suit the purpose, not generic filler.

**Individual Oral — B1 (visual stimulus) and B2 (conversation):**
- B1: describe visible details accurately, then connect them to the prescribed theme and explain why they matter.
- Separate what you can see from what you infer: “तस्वीर में…” for evidence; “शायद…” / “ऐसा लगता है कि…” for interpretation.
- B2: answer the teacher’s actual question, extend the answer with reasons/examples, and show more than one perspective when suitable.
- Keep the conversation connected to the theme; do not force unrelated memorised facts into it.

### Criterion C — Conceptual understanding (Paper 1) / Interactive skills (Individual Oral)

**Paper 1 — Conceptual understanding:** make the text type, audience, context and purpose recognisable through the choices you make.
- Identify who is writing, to whom, why, and in what situation before drafting.
- Use a format and tone that fit the task: for example, a formal letter/email differs from a personal diary or blog.
- Use text-type conventions selectively (title, greeting, sign-off, headings, speaker/audience address) when they fit the specified type.
- Do not assume a format label alone earns credit: the whole response should sound suited to its intended reader and purpose.
- Notice that text types can overlap; audience, context and purpose—not just the label—guide register and structure.

**Individual Oral — Interactive skills:** take part in a real exchange, not just a prepared monologue.
- Listen to the full question; answer it first, then elaborate.
- Respond to the examiner’s follow-up and build on what has already been discussed.
- Clarify or rephrase if needed; keep going if a word is missing.
- Balance speaking with listening; do not interrupt or give a long speech unrelated to the prompt.
- Show natural interaction with relevant detail, examples and appropriate turn-taking.

### “Criterion D” — personal final check, not an IB criterion here

Use this as a quick self-check after the official criteria:
- **D1 — Did I do the task?** Every bullet/question answered; no irrelevant memorised material.
- **D2 — Is it easy to follow?** Paragraphs/order and connectors make the line of thought clear.
- **D3 — Did I proofread?** Agreement, tense, spelling, punctuation and word count checked.
- **D4 — Does it sound appropriate?** Register, text-type conventions and idiom fit audience and purpose.

## Text-type and task checklist

1. Circle the **audience**, **purpose**, **context** and requested **text type** in the prompt.
2. Make a 3–4 point plan; match one point to each task bullet.
3. Choose register and opening/closing before drafting.
4. Include concrete examples or details, not only broad claims.
5. Leave time to check the response against every bullet and the word range.

For a formal letter/email, check recipient, subject (if appropriate), respectful greeting, clear request/purpose, organized details, courteous closing and name. For a blog/article, check a relevant title, reader-facing opening, clear paragraphs and a purposeful ending. Follow the exact task instructions if they differ from classroom templates.

## Common mistakes / examiner traps

- Treating “Criterion D” as a current IB Hindi B SL criterion without checking the component rubric.
- Using an impressive idiom incorrectly or in a tone-deaf context.
- Confusing **क्योंकि** (because) with **इसलिए** (therefore), or using connectors without a logical relationship.
- Writing a list of ideas without explaining or supporting them.
- Describing a visual stimulus without connecting it to the theme, or stating guesses as facts.
- Mixing formal and informal register; using आप and तुम for the same person without reason.
- Memorising an answer that does not address the task or follow-up question.
- Spending time on elaborate openings and leaving task bullets or proofreading unfinished.

## Self-test

1. Name the three criteria used for current Hindi B SL Paper 1 in this vault.
2. What is the difference between **क्योंकि** and **इसलिए**?
3. Correct the phrase: “परिवार के परंपरा” and “लड़कियाँ जाता है”.
4. Explain one safe way to use **कमर कसना** in a sentence.
5. In the Individual Oral, how do you distinguish a visible detail from an inference?

> [!question]- Answers
> 1. A Language, B Message, C Conceptual understanding. The Individual Oral uses A Language, B1 Message (visual stimulus), B2 Message (conversation), and C Interactive skills.
> 2. क्योंकि introduces a reason (“because”); इसलिए introduces a result (“therefore”).
> 3. परिवार **की** परंपरा; लड़कियाँ **जाती हैं**.
> 4. Example: परीक्षा की तैयारी के लिए मैंने कमर कस ली। It means to get ready / prepare seriously.
> 5. State evidence with “तस्वीर में…” and mark an interpretation with “शायद…”, “संभव है…” or “ऐसा लगता है कि…”.
