You receive three prompts drawn from the current unit's themes. Each specifies a text type. You choose **one** and produce a written response of **250–400 words**.

## Section 1.6 — Text Type: ईमेल (Email)

**Format:**

```
प्रेषक (From):
प्राप्तकर्ता (To):
विषय (Subject):

प्रिय ___ / आदरणीय ___ (salutation)

[Body paragraphs — usually 2–3]

धन्यवाद / भवदीय / आपका (closing)
[Your Name]
```

**Register.** Formal (आप) if to a teacher, principal, or authority figure. Semi-formal (आप or तुम depending on relationship) to peers or family. Concise and purposive — an email is not a letter or an essay; get to the point.

**Common purposes tested:** request permission, propose a plan, express concern, invite someone, follow up on an issue.

### Formula for a clean email

1. **Opening line — the ask or purpose** (1 sentence). "मैं आपको यह ईमेल… के बारे में लिख रहा/रही हूँ।"
2. **Context** (1 short paragraph). Why this matters.
3. **Detail / body** (1 paragraph). What you are proposing / requesting / reporting.
4. **Close** — thank + expected next step + polite sign-off.

## Section 1.7 — Text Type: डायरी (Diary entry)

**Format:**

```
दिनांक: __/__/____ (date)
दिन: (day, optional)
समय: (time, optional)

प्रिय डायरी, (optional address)

[Personal reflective body — first person, past or present tense depending on narrative]

[Your Name at end — optional in Hindi tradition]
```

**Register.** Personal, first-person, informal (मैं-focused). Emotional, introspective. Do NOT use "आप" — you are talking to yourself or the diary. Uses तुम/तू at most (if addressing the diary).

**Voice.** A diary earns marks for authenticity, not for polished argument. Include feelings (खुशी, दुख, गुस्सा, उलझन), specific sensory detail, small moments. Reflection over description.

### The three-move diary paragraph

1. **What happened** (event, specific).
2. **How I felt** (emotion, honest).
3. **What I think it means** (reflection, wisdom-seeking).

## Section 1.8 — Text Type: ब्लॉग (Blog)

**Format:**

```
शीर्षक: [Attention-grabbing title]

लेखक: [Your name] · दिनांक: __/__/____

[Engaging opening hook — a question, a scene, a claim]

[Sub-headings for sections — optional but recommended]

[Body — 2–3 paragraphs, conversational, opinionated]

[Ending: call for comments / like & share / question to reader]
```

**Register.** Conversational, opinionated, direct address to reader (आप / पाठकों). Personal voice with a public tilt. Uses "मैं" and "आप" together — I am talking to you.

**Blog voice moves.**

- Ask the reader a question early. ("क्या आपने कभी सोचा है…?")
- Use short, punchy sentences occasionally.
- Include one personal anecdote.
- End with an invitation to engage. ("नीचे कमेंट में बताइए…" / "अपने विचार साझा करें।")

## Section 1.9 — Text Type: लेख (Article)

**Format:**

```
शीर्षक (title)

लेखक का नाम (byline)

प्रस्तावना (introduction — hook + thesis)

विषय-वस्तु (body — argument-driven, 2–3 paragraphs with topic sentences)

निष्कर्ष (conclusion — restate + call to reflection)
```

**Register.** Formal, third-person, well-structured argument. Statistics, examples, or expert reference strengthen. Opinion is stated but supported with reasoning, not asserted alone.

**Structure discipline.**

- Introduction ≠ diary opener. State the issue, then your stance in one sentence.
- Every body paragraph has a topic sentence naming its claim.
- Conclusion ≠ summary. It restates the thesis in richer terms and gestures at implications.

An article is what you would find in *Dainik Bhaskar*, *Amar Ujala*, or *India Today* Hindi editions — not a personal essay.

## Section 1.10 — Text Type: प्रस्ताव (Proposal)

**Format:**

```
शीर्षक: [Title of proposal]

प्रस्तुतकर्ता: [Your name / your group]
प्राप्तकर्ता: [The person or body you are proposing to]
दिनांक: __/__/____

पृष्ठभूमि (background — what problem exists)

उद्देश्य (objectives — what this proposal aims to achieve)

कार्य-योजना (action plan — steps, in order)

बजट / संसाधन / समय-सीमा (budget / resources / timeline — as relevant)

अपेक्षित परिणाम (expected outcomes)

निष्कर्ष (conclusion / request for approval)
```

**Register.** Formal, structured, persuasive. Bullet-friendly. The audience is a decision-maker (principal, teacher, committee). Your goal: get approval.

**Persuasion moves.**

- Name the problem in one sentence before offering the solution.
- Objectives should be specific and measurable.
- Timeline should be realistic — the reader is checking feasibility.
- Close with a specific ask ("मैं आपसे इस प्रस्ताव को स्वीकृति देने का अनुरोध करता/करती हूँ।").

## Section 1.11 — Grammar Hotspots

- **Verb agreement** — matches gender AND number of subject. लड़का जाता है (m.sg.) / लड़की जाती है (f.sg.) / लड़के जाते हैं (m.pl.) / लड़कियाँ जाती हैं (f.pl.).
- **Tenses** — वर्तमान (present), भूत (past — सामान्य, अपूर्ण, पूर्ण), भविष्य (future). Diary usually mixes past narrative with present reflection. Article usually stays in present.
- **Postpositions (परसर्ग)** — का/की/के, को, में, पर, से. Silent-error factory. का/की/के agrees with gender/number of the NEXT noun, not the previous one.
- **Register — आप vs तुम vs तू.** Wrong register in a formal email = format mark lost. आप = respectful/formal (elders, teachers, strangers). तुम = friendly/informal (peers, siblings). तू = intimate/close (very close friends, family) or rude in the wrong context.
- **कारक system** — the case system that determines verb agreement in past-tense transitive verbs. Ergative "ने" construction: मैंने पत्र लिखा (I wrote a letter). Verb agrees with the object, not the subject.
- **Compound verbs** — जा रहा हूँ (continuous), जा चुका हूँ (perfect), जाना है (obligation). Get these right; they carry a lot of meaning.
- **Word order** — Hindi is SOV (Subject-Object-Verb): मैं (S) खाना (O) खाता हूँ (V). The verb goes at the end.

## Section 1.12 — Paper 2 Reading Strategy

Three texts, ~45 questions, one hour.

### Question types

- **Multiple choice (बहुविकल्पीय)** — one correct interpretation among four.
- **Short answer (लघु उत्तर)** — usually 1–2 sentence responses in Hindi. Answer *in your own words* where instructed — copying full sentences from the text often loses marks.
- **True/False with justification (सत्य/असत्य कारण सहित)** — you must quote the exact line from the text that justifies your answer. **No quote = no mark**, even if T/F is correct.
- **Vocabulary matching (शब्दार्थ)** — pair Hindi words in the text with synonyms or definitions.
- **Reference identification (संदर्भ पहचान)** — यह / वह / उन्होंने refers to whom? Requires close reading.
- **Table / grid completion (तालिका पूर्ति)** — fill facts from the text into a structured grid.

### The strategy

1. Skim all three texts first (5 min) to gauge length and difficulty; **do the easiest one first**.
2. Read the questions *before* re-reading the text closely — you scan more effectively when you know what you're hunting.
3. Underline evidence as you read. Every T/F answer's justification quote gets a specific mark under "referencing".
4. Do not leave blanks — an educated guess on MCQ is free expected value.
5. Watch the direction: **"पाठ के अनुसार"** (according to the text) means only what the text says — no outside knowledge, no personal opinion.

## Section 1.13 — Exam-Ready Vocabulary and Phrases

### Opening a written text

- मैं आपको यह ईमेल लिख रहा/रही हूँ… (I am writing this email to you…)
- आज मैं आपके साथ… के बारे में साझा करना चाहता/चाहती हूँ। (Today I want to share about… with you.)
- क्या आपने कभी सोचा है कि… (Have you ever thought that…?)
- हमारे समाज में एक बड़ी समस्या है… (There is a big problem in our society…)

### Presenting an argument

- सबसे पहले, / दूसरे, / अंत में (Firstly / Secondly / Finally)
- इसका मुख्य कारण यह है कि… (The main reason for this is…)
- उदाहरण के लिए, / जैसे कि (For example, / such as)
- इस दृष्टि से देखा जाए तो… (Viewed from this perspective…)
- हालाँकि / फिर भी / लेकिन (However / nevertheless / but)

### Closing

- अंत में, मैं यह कहना चाहता/चाहती हूँ कि… (In conclusion, I want to say that…)
- मुझे आशा है कि… (I hope that…)
- अपने विचार अवश्य साझा करें। (Do share your thoughts.)
- धन्यवाद / भवदीय (Thank you / Yours sincerely)

### Handy connectors and intensifiers

- क्योंकि (because), इसलिए (therefore), अगर (if), तो (then)
- बहुत, अत्यंत, वास्तव में, सच में (very, extremely, really, truly)
- साथ ही, इसके अलावा, ऊपर से (also, in addition, on top of that)
